Core message · Unit 5
Knowing what is right is common; saying it to someone with power over you is the leadership skill.
  • Most ethical failures in organisations are not failures of knowledge: people usually sense that something is wrong.
  • The tension arises when doing the right thing costs something — a deadline, a bonus, a relationship with a superior — and when the dilemma is right against right rather than right against wrong.
  • This unit trains moral courage as a practised skill: diagnosing the dilemma, scripting the voice and rehearsing the challenge upward.

Driving question

What would it cost you to challenge your CEO — and what would it cost if you stayed silent?

Unit content (course outline)

  • “Ethical Leadership: Doing things right vs. doing the right thing” - Wrap-up of Boostcamp Episode
  • From compliance to moral courage: what happens when doing the right thing costs you something
  • Real cases of ethical leadership under organizational pressure
  • The courage to challenge upward as an explicit leadership skill

AI integration in this unit

AI enters this unit twice. As a sparring partner, a generative AI tool plays the sceptical senior leader so that students can rehearse objections before the role play; it never writes the final script. As a subject, AI raises its own ethical questions: who is responsible when an algorithm contributes to harm? Students apply the EU AI Act risk tiers, the HLEG requirements for trustworthy AI and the responsibility gap to the case. The decision to speak up, and the words used, stay human; every AI contribution is declared.
