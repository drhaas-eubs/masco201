Core message · Unit 4
Most leadership failures in a crisis come from solving the wrong kind of problem quickly and confidently.
  • Under pressure, leaders reach for technical fixes because they are fast, visible and within their authority.
  • Yet the hardest problems require people to change values, habits or loyalties, and no expert can do that work for them.
  • This unit trains you to diagnose the problem type, choose a decision mode that fits the context, and keep trust while you adapt.
Intended learning outcomes addressed
1Critically discuss the approaches to contemporary strategic leadership in organizations.
4Demonstrate the significance of leading strategically during periods of change and turbulence.

Driving question

Is the problem in front of you one that an expert can solve — or one that only the people who have it can work out?

Unit content (course outline)

  • “Under Pressure: Mastering Decision-Making in Demanding Circumstances” - Wrap-up of Boostcamp Episode
  • The technical vs. adaptive problem distinction as a practical diagnostic tool
  • Case-based exercise: real leadership failures caused by treating an adaptive problem as technical
  • The Cynefin Framework for decision-making under uncertainty
  • How leaders communicate and maintain trust when things go wrong

AI integration in this unit

In a crisis AI can summarise incoming information, model scenarios and red-team a plan within minutes. It cannot carry the loss, mobilise people to change or take responsibility for the call. In the Crisis Room students set a level of automation for each decision, use AI as an adversarial pre-mortem partner, and check whether a tool fits the Cynefin domain they are in. AI drafts, the leader decides, and every AI contribution is declared.
