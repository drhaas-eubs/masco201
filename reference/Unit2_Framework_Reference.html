Slibrary 3 — Authentic Leadership & Identity4 frameworks

F09 · Slibrary 3 · Sheet 1/4

True North — Authentic Leadership

Lead from your life story, tested values and an integrated life

Source (Harvard): George, B., Sims, P., McLean, A.N. and Mayer, D. (2007) 'Discovering your authentic leadership', Harvard Business Review, 85(2), pp. 129-138.

In one sentenceYou become an effective leader not by copying others but by understanding your own life story, testing your values under pressure and building people around you who tell you the truth.
About the author(s)Bill George was chairman and CEO of Medtronic and later a senior fellow and professor of management practice at Harvard Business School. His co-authors included Peter Sims, with whom he wrote True North (2007).
Guiding question (Clara Ruiz at Valtera Foods case)Clara Ruiz describes herself as 'direct, fair and driven by quality'. Which dimension of True North does her 360 result challenge, and who on her support team could tell her the truth before her plan is due in four weeks?
F10 · Slibrary 3 · Sheet 2/4

Crucibles of Leadership

Defining ordeals that leaders turn into meaning and adaptive capacity

Source (Harvard): Bennis, W.G. and Thomas, R.J. (2002) 'Crucibles of leadership', Harvard Business Review, 80(9), pp. 39-45.

In one sentenceHard experiences do not automatically make someone a leader; what matters is the meaning the person draws from them and the ability to adapt that this builds.
About the author(s)Warren Bennis (1925–2014) was Distinguished Professor of Business Administration at the University of Southern California and a founder of leadership studies. Robert J. Thomas was executive director of the Accenture Institute for Strategic Change.
Guiding question (Clara Ruiz at Valtera Foods case)Could Clara's 360 feedback at Valtera Foods become a crucible for her? What meaning would she need to make of it for the experience to build adaptive capacity rather than defensiveness?
F11 · Slibrary 3 · Sheet 3/4

Leadership Circle Profile

A 360 that links creative competencies to the reactive habits beneath them

Source (Harvard): Anderson, R.J. and Adams, W.A. (2016) Mastering Leadership: An Integrated Framework for Breakthrough Performance and Extraordinary Business Results. Hoboken, NJ: Wiley.

In one sentenceThe profile shows a leader's strengths in the top half of a circle and the fear-driven habits that limit them in the bottom half, so feedback explains not only what to improve but why.
About the author(s)Bob Anderson founded The Leadership Circle and developed the Leadership Circle Profile. Bill Adams co-founded the Full Circle Group, a leadership consultancy; together they wrote Mastering Leadership (2016) and Scaling Leadership (2019).
Guiding question (Clara Ruiz at Valtera Foods case)Clara rates herself high on relating; her eight direct reports rate her low on relating and listening. Which reactive tendency below the line best explains that gap — and her eleven line stops?
F12 · Slibrary 3 · Sheet 4/4

Schwartz Theory of Basic Values

Ten universal values arranged in a circle of compatibility and conflict

Source (Harvard): Schwartz, S.H. (2012) 'An overview of the Schwartz theory of basic values', Online Readings in Psychology and Culture, 2(1), article 11.

In one sentencePeople everywhere hold the same ten basic values in different priorities; values next to each other fit together, and values on opposite sides of the circle pull leaders in different directions.
About the author(s)Shalom H. Schwartz is Professor Emeritus of Psychology at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem. His values theory is one of the most widely used in cross-cultural psychology and informs the European Social Survey.
Guiding question (Clara Ruiz at Valtera Foods case)Clara's eleven line stops show that achievement and security drive her decisions at Valtera. Which value on the opposite side of the circle do her direct reports seem to miss, and how would she show it?

Slibrary 4 — AI as Reflection Partner4 frameworks

F13 · Slibrary 4 · Sheet 1/4

Gibbs' Reflective Cycle with AI

Six reflective stages; AI asks the questions, the student writes the answers

Source (Harvard): Gibbs, G. (1988) Learning by Doing: A Guide to Teaching and Learning Methods. Oxford: Further Education Unit, Oxford Polytechnic.

In one sentenceWork through six steps — what happened, how you felt, what went well or badly, why, what else you could have done and what you will do next — with AI asking the questions and you writing the answers.
About the author(s)A course adaptation by Dr. Hildegard Haas, EU Business School. Graham Gibbs was a British educational developer whose 1988 guide Learning by Doing, written at Oxford Polytechnic, introduced the widely used reflective cycle.
Guiding question (Clara Ruiz at Valtera Foods case)Take Clara through one full turn of the cycle for the moment she pasted her 360 comments into an AI assistant. At which stage did she skip ahead, and what would the analysis stage have asked her?
F14 · Slibrary 4 · Sheet 2/4

Sycophancy Check

Test whether AI feedback agrees with you because it is true

Source (Harvard): Sharma, M., Tong, M., Korbak, T., Duvenaud, D., Askell, A., Bowman, S.R. et al. (2024) 'Towards understanding sycophancy in language models', International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR 2024).

In one sentenceAI tools often agree with whatever view you hint at, so before trusting a comfortable answer, ask for the opposite case, check the evidence and decide for yourself.
About the author(s)A course adaptation by Dr. Hildegard Haas, EU Business School, of research led by Mrinank Sharma with colleagues at Anthropic and the University of Oxford, presented at the International Conference on Learning Representations in 2024.
Guiding question (Clara Ruiz at Valtera Foods case)Clara asked an AI assistant whether her team's comments were 'a normal reaction to a demanding manager'. How did that wording invite sycophancy, and how should she rephrase the question?
F15 · Slibrary 4 · Sheet 3/4

Disclosure Boundary

Decide what never goes into an AI tool before you reflect

Source (Harvard): European Parliament and Council of the European Union (2016) Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (General Data Protection Regulation). Official Journal of the European Union, L 119, 4 May.

In one sentenceBefore you ask AI to help you reflect, decide what you may share, what you must anonymise and what you must never enter — especially sensitive data and anything identifying other people.
About the author(s)A course adaptation by Dr. Hildegard Haas, EU Business School, of the General Data Protection Regulation, the EU law on personal data adopted in 2016 and applicable since 25 May 2018.
Guiding question (Clara Ruiz at Valtera Foods case)Clara pasted her team's anonymised 360 comments into an AI assistant. Were eight comments from a small team at the Valencia plant truly anonymous, and what should she have removed first?
F16 · Slibrary 4 · Sheet 4/4

AI-Assisted 360 Synthesis

AI groups anonymised 360 comments; the leader interprets the pattern

Source (Harvard): Anderson, R.J. and Adams, W.A. (2016) Mastering Leadership: An Integrated Framework for Breakthrough Performance and Extraordinary Business Results. Hoboken, NJ: Wiley.

In one sentenceLet AI sort anonymised 360 comments into themes, then compare how you see yourself with how others see you, and decide yourself what the gaps mean and which two to work on.
About the author(s)A course adaptation by Dr. Hildegard Haas, EU Business School, drawing on the Leadership Circle Profile by Bob Anderson and Bill Adams and on research on self–other agreement in 360-degree feedback.
Guiding question (Clara Ruiz at Valtera Foods case)Run Clara's eight 360 responses through the five steps. Which two gaps would you put into her development plan due in four weeks, and which interpretation must she make herself rather than leave to AI?
