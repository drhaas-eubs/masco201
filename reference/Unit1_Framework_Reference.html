Slibrary 1 — Leadership Theory, Critically Reviewed4 frameworks

F01 · Slibrary 1 · Sheet 1/4

Evolution of Leadership Theories

Five research eras, five different questions about leadership

Source (Harvard): Yukl, G.A. and Gardner, W.L. (2020) Leadership in Organizations. 9th edn, Global edn. Harlow: Pearson.

In one sentenceLeadership theory has moved from asking who leaders are, to what they do, to when a style works, to how they change followers and finally to how groups lead together through change.
About the author(s)Gary Yukl is Professor Emeritus at the University at Albany, State University of New York, and one of the most cited scholars of managerial leadership. William L. Gardner joined him as co-author of the ninth edition of Leadership in Organizations.
Guiding question (Nordhafen Logistics case)Nordhafen's original 2019 role description for Pieter reads like a list of tasks. Which era of leadership theory does that description belong to, and which question from a later era does Anna's board now have to answer?
F02 · Slibrary 1 · Sheet 2/4

Yukl's Hierarchical Taxonomy of Leader Behaviour

Four meta-categories that organise what effective leaders actually do

Source (Harvard): Yukl, G. (2012) 'Effective leadership behavior: what we know and what questions need more attention', Academy of Management Perspectives, 26(4), pp. 66-85.

In one sentenceEffective leaders do four kinds of things — get the task done, look after people, drive change and manage the outside world — and must shift between them as the situation changes.
About the author(s)Gary Yukl is Professor Emeritus at the University at Albany, State University of New York. His textbook Leadership in Organizations, first published in 1981, has been a standard graduate text for four decades and is the required reading for this course.
Guiding question (Nordhafen Logistics case)The AI system at Nordhafen now performs most of Pieter's task-oriented behaviours. Which relations, change and external behaviours — visible in the turnover rise, the works council request and the Rotterdam customer — would the new role still need?
F03 · Slibrary 1 · Sheet 3/4

Manager vs. Leader

Coping with complexity versus coping with change

Source (Harvard): Kotter, J.P. (1990) 'What leaders really do', Harvard Business Review, 68(3), pp. 103-111.

In one sentenceManagement keeps a complex organisation running through plans, structures and controls; leadership moves it through change by setting direction, aligning people and inspiring them — and every organisation needs both.
About the author(s)John P. Kotter is Konosuke Matsushita Professor of Leadership, Emeritus, at Harvard Business School and the author of Leading Change (1996). Abraham Zaleznik (1924–2011) was a Harvard Business School professor and practising psychoanalyst.
Guiding question (Nordhafen Logistics case)Katrin's proposal treats Pieter's role as management that the AI dashboard can replace. Using the three paired functions, what share of the Dutch regional role is leadership, and can €140,000 a year buy that back later?
F04 · Slibrary 1 · Sheet 4/4

Bass's Four I's of Transformational Leadership

Influence, inspiration, stimulation and consideration beyond self-interest

Source (Harvard): Bass, B.M. and Riggio, R.E. (2006) Transformational Leadership. 2nd edn. Mahwah, NJ: Lawrence Erlbaum Associates.

In one sentenceTransformational leaders earn trust as role models, offer a compelling vision, challenge people to think and care for each person — and this lifts performance above what fair rewards alone achieve.
About the author(s)Bernard M. Bass (1925–2007) was Distinguished Professor of Management at Binghamton University, State University of New York. Ronald E. Riggio is Professor of Leadership and Organizational Psychology at Claremont McKenna College.
Guiding question (Nordhafen Logistics case)Pieter's personal visit kept Nordhafen's Rotterdam customer. Which of the Four I's did that visit draw on, and which could a dashboard reporting every Monday at 06:00 never supply?

Slibrary 2 — AI and the Leader's Work4 frameworks

F05 · Slibrary 2 · Sheet 1/4

Task-Based AI Exposure

Jobs are bundles of tasks; AI exposure is rated task by task

Source (Harvard): Eloundou, T., Manning, S., Mishkin, P. and Rock, D. (2024) 'GPTs are GPTs: labor market impact potential of LLMs', Science, 384(6702), pp. 1306-1308.

In one sentenceAI does not replace whole jobs at once: it changes individual tasks, so the useful question is which of a role's tasks AI can speed up and which still need a person.
About the author(s)Tyna Eloundou and Pamela Mishkin are researchers at OpenAI; Sam Manning researches the economics of AI; Daniel Rock is an assistant professor at the Wharton School, University of Pennsylvania. An earlier version circulated in 2023 as 'GPTs are GPTs'.
Guiding question (Nordhafen Logistics case)List the tasks in Pieter's role and rate each for AI exposure. If rostering, forecasting and reporting are exposed, which unexposed tasks explain the turnover rise from 14 % to 23 % at the two Dutch sites?
F06 · Slibrary 2 · Sheet 2/4

Jagged Technological Frontier

AI excels at some tasks and fails at similar-looking neighbours

Source (Harvard): Dell'Acqua, F., McFowland III, E., Mollick, E.R., Lifshitz-Assaf, H., Kellogg, K., Rajendran, S., Krayer, L., Candelon, F. and Lakhani, K.R. (2023) Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier: Field Experimental Evidence of the Effects of AI on Knowledge Worker Productivity and Quality. Harvard Business School Working Paper No. 24-013.

In one sentenceAI is very good at some tasks and quietly wrong at others that look just as easy, so the gain depends on knowing where that invisible line runs.
About the author(s)Fabrizio Dell'Acqua is a researcher at Harvard Business School's Laboratory for Innovation Science; co-authors include Ethan Mollick (Wharton), Karim Lakhani (Harvard Business School), Katherine Kellogg (MIT Sloan) and François Candelon (then BCG Henderson Institute).
Guiding question (Nordhafen Logistics case)Nordhafen's planning system clearly works for forecasting. Is judging what the works council and the Rotterdam customer need inside or outside the frontier, and what would Katrin's dashboard show if it fell outside?
F07 · Slibrary 2 · Sheet 3/4

Moravec's Paradox

Hard for humans is often easy for machines — and vice versa

Source (Harvard): Moravec, H. (1988) Mind Children: The Future of Robot and Human Intelligence. Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Press.

In one sentenceComputers find maths and search easy but find what any person does without thinking — perceiving, sensing and reading other people — very hard, which is why those skills keep their value.
About the author(s)Hans Moravec (born 1948) is an Austrian-born roboticist who worked at the Robotics Institute of Carnegie Mellon University, where he researched mobile robots and wrote Mind Children and Robot (1999).
Guiding question (Nordhafen Logistics case)Which of Pieter's daily activities at Nordhafen fall in the Moravec zone — easy for him, hard for the planning system — and how should that change the role profile Anna has asked HR to rewrite?
F08 · Slibrary 2 · Sheet 4/4

Leadership Task × AI Map

Yukl's four behaviour families mapped against what AI can carry

Source (Harvard): Yukl, G. (2012) 'Effective leadership behavior: what we know and what questions need more attention', Academy of Management Perspectives, 26(4), pp. 66-85.

In one sentenceFor each kind of leadership behaviour, decide how much AI can help — a lot with tasks, some with change and external scanning, almost none with relationships — and protect what remains human.
About the author(s)A course adaptation by Dr. Hildegard Haas, EU Business School, of the taxonomy by Gary Yukl, Professor Emeritus at the University at Albany, State University of New York, and author of Leadership in Organizations.
Guiding question (Nordhafen Logistics case)Draw the map for Nordhafen's Dutch region. If the AI carries most task behaviours, which relations and external behaviours must appear in the new role profile before the board decides in three weeks?
