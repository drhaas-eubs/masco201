F49 · Slibrary 13 · Sheet 1/4

French & Raven's Power Bases — Leader Lens

Six sources of influence — and which ones survive without a title

Source (Harvard): French, J.R.P. and Raven, B. (1959) 'The bases of social power', in Cartwright, D. (ed.) Studies in Social Power. Ann Arbor, MI: Institute for Social Research, pp. 150-167.

In one sentencePeople follow you for different reasons: because of your role, what you can give or take away, what you know, who you are, or the information you share — only the last three come with you when the title is missing.
About the author(s)John R.P. French and Bertram H. Raven were social psychologists at the University of Michigan's Research Center for Group Dynamics when they published the typology in 1959. Raven, who later spent his career at the University of California, Los Angeles, extended it with informational power in 1965.
Guiding question (Nordlicht Logistics case)Lena Hartmann has expert and informational power but almost no referent power with Nordlicht's three depot managers: which base can she build in the two weeks before her ten-minute slot, and what would borrowing the director's legitimate power cost her?
F50 · Slibrary 13 · Sheet 2/4

Aristotle's Rhetorical Triangle — Ethos, Pathos, Logos

Character, emotion and reason — the three artistic proofs

Source (Harvard): Aristotle (1991) On Rhetoric: A Theory of Civic Discourse. Translated by G.A. Kennedy. New York: Oxford University Press.

In one sentenceA message convinces when the audience trusts the speaker, feels why it matters to them and can follow the argument; relying on only one of these three makes it sound like manipulation or a lecture.
About the author(s)Aristotle (384–322 BCE) was a Greek philosopher, a student of Plato and tutor to Alexander the Great, who founded the Lyceum in Athens. His Rhetoric is the founding systematic treatment of persuasion; it survives as lecture material rather than a finished book.
Guiding question (Nordlicht Logistics case)Lena's case at Nordlicht is strong on logos — 18% late deliveries cut to under 8% and €640,000 a year saved — but how can she build ethos with Jens and pathos for Marta, whose depot would lose most autonomy?
F51 · Slibrary 13 · Sheet 3/4

Covey's Four Cores of Credibility

Integrity, intent, capabilities, results — the roots and fruit of trust

Source (Harvard): Covey, S.M.R. (2006) The Speed of Trust: The One Thing That Changes Everything. New York: Free Press.

In one sentencePeople trust you when they see your honesty, believe your motives, rate your abilities and can point to your results; the first two are character, the last two competence, and a gap in any one costs trust.
About the author(s)Stephen M.R. Covey is an American author and former chief executive of the Covey Leadership Center, which merged into FranklinCovey in 1997. He co-founded the firm's Speed of Trust practice and later wrote Trust and Inspire (2022), listed in this course's bibliography.
Guiding question (Nordlicht Logistics case)Jens Albrecht calls Lena 'a consultant who has never loaded a lorry': which of Covey's four cores is he questioning, and what could Lena show in a day at each Nordlicht depot to strengthen it before the meeting?
F52 · Slibrary 13 · Sheet 4/4

Kouzes & Posner's Five Practices

Five observable behaviours of leaders at their personal best

Source (Harvard): Kouzes, J.M. and Posner, B.Z. (2017) The Leadership Challenge. 6th edn. Hoboken, NJ: Wiley.

In one sentenceExemplary leaders set the example, share an inspiring vision, challenge how things are done, help others act and recognise their contributions — and none of it works unless they do what they say they will do.
About the author(s)James M. Kouzes and Barry Z. Posner are American leadership researchers associated with the Leavey School of Business at Santa Clara University, where Posner is professor of leadership and served as dean. Their book The Leadership Challenge has appeared in several editions since 1987.
Guiding question (Nordlicht Logistics case)Which of the five practices could Lena show to Nordlicht's depot managers before she asks for anything — and which practice would she violate if the managing director imposed the planning tool on her behalf?

Slibrary 14 — AI: Presence in AI-Mediated Communication4 frameworks

F53 · Slibrary 14 · Sheet 1/4

Social Presence Theory

How much of the other person comes through the medium

Source (Harvard): Short, J., Williams, E. and Christie, B. (1976) The Social Psychology of Telecommunications. London: Wiley.

In one sentenceSome channels let people feel you are really there and others do not; the harder or more personal the message, the more of your presence it needs.
About the author(s)John Short, Ederyn Williams and Bruce Christie were British researchers who studied telecommunications use in organisations during the 1970s. Their 1976 book is the origin of the concept of social presence, widely used in later research on online learning and computer-mediated communication.
Guiding question (Nordlicht Logistics case)A colleague suggests Lena send Nordlicht's depot managers an AI-produced video summary in advance: what social presence would that carry compared with a day at each depot, and which would build more credibility?
F54 · Slibrary 14 · Sheet 2/4

Hyperpersonal Model

When the mediated impression becomes better than reality

Source (Harvard): Walther, J.B. (1996) 'Computer-mediated communication: impersonal, interpersonal, and hyperpersonal interaction', Communication Research, 23(1), pp. 3-43.

In one sentenceOnline, people can show their best side and others fill in the gaps generously, so the impression can be better than reality — until the two meet in person.
About the author(s)Joseph B. Walther is an American communication scholar known for social information processing theory and the hyperpersonal model; he has held professorships at several US universities, including Michigan State University and the University of California, Santa Barbara.
Guiding question (Nordlicht Logistics case)An AI-polished presentation might impress Nordlicht's depot managers on screen: what happens to Lena's credibility when Pieter de Vries later meets her on the depot floor in Rotterdam and the polish does not match her practice?
F55 · Slibrary 14 · Sheet 3/4

The Liar's Dividend

When fakes are possible, the truth can be denied

Source (Harvard): Chesney, R. and Citron, D. (2019) 'Deep fakes: a looming challenge for privacy, democracy, and national security', California Law Review, 107(6), pp. 1753-1820.

In one sentenceBecause fake videos and voices are easy to make, people can now claim that real evidence is fake too, so a leader's trustworthiness rests more and more on a consistent record.
About the author(s)Robert Chesney is an American law professor at the University of Texas at Austin; Danielle Keats Citron, a law professor at the University of Virginia and a 2019 MacArthur Fellow, is known for her work on privacy and online harms. Both write on technology, law and security.
Guiding question (Nordlicht Logistics case)If Lena sent an AI-produced video in which a synthetic voice presents her proposal, how might that affect whether Nordlicht's depot managers believe her own words later — and what disclosure would protect her credibility?
F56 · Slibrary 14 · Sheet 4/4

AI Rehearsal Loop

AI as sparring partner in rehearsal — never on stage

Source (Harvard): Ericsson, K.A., Krampe, R.T. and Tesch-Römer, C. (1993) 'The role of deliberate practice in the acquisition of expert performance', Psychological Review, 100(3), pp. 363-406.

In one sentenceUse AI to play the toughest critic while you rehearse, then present to real people and compare what you intended with what they actually took away.
About the author(s)The loop is a course adaptation by Dr. Hildegard Haas. Its basis is the work of K. Anders Ericsson (1947–2020), a Swedish psychologist at Florida State University whose research on deliberate practice shaped the study of expert performance.
Guiding question (Nordlicht Logistics case)Which three questions would an AI sceptic predict from Jens, Marta and Pieter about Nordlicht's shared planning tool — and which of them can only be tested by Lena standing in front of the depot managers?
