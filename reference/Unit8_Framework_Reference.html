F57 · Slibrary 15 · Sheet 1/4

Self-Determination Theory

Autonomy, competence and relatedness fuel lasting motivation

Source (Harvard): Ryan, R.M. and Deci, E.L. (2000) 'Self-determination theory and the facilitation of intrinsic motivation, social development, and well-being', American Psychologist, 55(1), pp. 68-78.

In one sentencePeople stay motivated when they have a say in their work, feel they are getting better at it and feel connected to others; take these away and motivation turns into pressure or disengagement.
About the author(s)Edward L. Deci and Richard M. Ryan developed self-determination theory at the University of Rochester. Ryan is now at the Institute for Positive Psychology and Education, Australian Catholic University, and professor emeritus at Rochester; Deci is professor emeritus at Rochester.
Guiding question (Velatra Insurance case)At Velatra, sick days in Giulia Ferri's team are up by a third and 'my work is meaningful' scores are the lowest in the division: which of the three needs has the claims-triage system thwarted most, and how could her recommendation restore it?
F58 · Slibrary 15 · Sheet 2/4

Bridges' Transition Model

Change is the event; transition is the inner journey

Source (Harvard): Bridges, W. (1991) Managing Transitions: Making the Most of Change. Reading, MA: Addison-Wesley.

In one sentenceA change can be announced in a day, but people need time to let go of the old, cope with an uncertain in-between period and only then commit to the new.
About the author(s)William Bridges (1933–2013) was an American author and consultant who taught English literature before turning to the study of personal and organisational transitions. His books Transitions and Managing Transitions remain widely used in change management.
Guiding question (Velatra Insurance case)One Velatra handler says, 'I used to be the expert. Now I check the computer's homework': what ending has Giulia's team not acknowledged, and how long might their neutral zone last before the health-claims roll-out next year?
F59 · Slibrary 15 · Sheet 3/4

Kotter's 8-Step Change Model

From urgency to culture — eight steps that cannot be skipped

Source (Harvard): Kotter, J.P. (1996) Leading Change. Boston, MA: Harvard Business School Press.

In one sentenceBig changes succeed when leaders create urgency, build a strong team, share a clear vision, clear the way, show early wins and make the new way part of the culture — in that order.
About the author(s)John P. Kotter is professor emeritus at Harvard Business School and one of the most widely read authors on leadership and change. He also co-founded the consulting firm Kotter International.
Guiding question (Velatra Insurance case)Velatra's board wants the claims system extended to health claims next year: which of Kotter's eight steps were skipped in the motor and home roll-out, and which must Giulia insist on before her team shrinks from 14 to nine?
F60 · Slibrary 15 · Sheet 4/4

Job Crafting

Employees reshape tasks, relationships and meaning from below

Source (Harvard): Wrzesniewski, A. and Dutton, J.E. (2001) 'Crafting a job: revisioning employees as active crafters of their work', Academy of Management Review, 26(2), pp. 179-201.

In one sentencePeople can make their own jobs more meaningful by changing what they do, whom they work with and how they think about their work — and leaders can invite them to.
About the author(s)Amy Wrzesniewski is an organisational psychologist at the Wharton School, University of Pennsylvania, previously at Yale School of Management. Jane E. Dutton is a professor emerita at the University of Michigan's Ross School of Business and a founder of positive organisational scholarship.
Guiding question (Velatra Insurance case)With around 70% of Velatra's claims processed automatically, how could Giulia invite her handlers to craft new tasks, relationships and meaning — for example around complex claims like the €38,000 Bergamo case — rather than simply reducing the team?

Slibrary 16 — AI: Human–AI Collaboration & Oversight4 frameworks

F61 · Slibrary 16 · Sheet 1/4

Centaurs and Cyborgs

Divide the work with AI, or weave it together

Source (Harvard): Dell'Acqua, F., McFowland III, E., Mollick, E.R., Lifshitz-Assaf, H., Kellogg, K., Rajendran, S., Krayer, L., Candelon, F. and Lakhani, K.R. (2023) Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier: Field Experimental Evidence of the Effects of AI on Knowledge Worker Productivity and Quality. Harvard Business School Working Paper No. 24-013.

In one sentenceSome people split work with AI into clear human and machine parts, others mix the two at every step; leaders must decide which tasks may be mixed and which must stay human.
About the author(s)Fabrizio Dell'Acqua is a researcher at Harvard Business School; Ethan Mollick is a professor at the Wharton School, University of Pennsylvania, and author of Co-Intelligence; Karim Lakhani is a professor at Harvard Business School. The study was run with Boston Consulting Group.
Guiding question (Velatra Insurance case)At Velatra, should Giulia's handlers work as centaurs, reviewing only flagged claims, or as cyborgs on complex cases like the €38,000 Bergamo claim — and which decisions must never be interwoven with the model?
F62 · Slibrary 16 · Sheet 2/4

Human Oversight — EU AI Act Art. 14

High-risk AI must stay understandable, overridable and stoppable

Source (Harvard): European Parliament and Council of the European Union (2024) Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 laying down harmonised rules on artificial intelligence (Artificial Intelligence Act). Official Journal of the European Union, L, 12 July 2024.

In one sentenceEU law requires that people overseeing high-risk AI understand its limits, guard against over-trusting it, read its outputs correctly and are able to overrule or stop it.
About the author(s)Article 14 was adopted by the European Parliament and the Council of the European Union, the EU's two co-legislators, as part of the AI Act. The Act sorts AI systems into risk tiers; oversight duties for deployers are set out alongside it in Article 26.
Guiding question (Velatra Insurance case)Velatra's policy counts every overruling of the claims model against the team's efficiency: does that practice support or undermine effective human oversight of decisions like the €38,000 Bergamo rejection, and what should Giulia propose instead?
F63 · Slibrary 16 · Sheet 3/4

Meaningful Human Control

Tracking and tracing: real control, not a human rubber stamp

Source (Harvard): Santoni de Sio, F. and van den Hoven, J. (2018) 'Meaningful human control over autonomous systems: a philosophical account', Frontiers in Robotics and AI, 5, article 15.

In one sentenceHumans are really in control of an AI system only if it follows their reasons and if someone who understands the outcome can be held responsible for it.
About the author(s)Filippo Santoni de Sio and Jeroen van den Hoven are philosophers in the Ethics and Philosophy of Technology section of Delft University of Technology. Van den Hoven is known for work on value-sensitive design; Santoni de Sio for work on responsibility and AI.
Guiding question (Velatra Insurance case)The documentation of Velatra's claims model does not explain which factors drove the score: can Marco, Giulia or the COO meaningfully track or trace the €38,000 rejection — and who should answer for it?
F64 · Slibrary 16 · Sheet 4/4

De Cremer's AI-Savvy Leader

AI adoption is a leadership decision, not a technology project

Source (Harvard): De Cremer, D. (2024) The AI-Savvy Leader: Nine Ways to Take Back Control and Make AI Work. Boston, MA: Harvard Business Review Press.

In one sentenceLeaders, not technologists, must decide why and how AI is used, understand it well enough to ask good questions and stay responsible for what it does to people.
About the author(s)David De Cremer is a Belgian behavioural scientist working on trust, ethics and leadership. He has held professorships at NUS Business School, Singapore, and the University of Cambridge, and is a former Dean of the D'Amore-McKim School of Business, Northeastern University.
Guiding question (Velatra Insurance case)Velatra's COO frames the claims model as 96% accurate and the team as slowing it down: what purpose and accountability questions would an AI-savvy leader ask before cutting Giulia's team from 14 handlers to nine?
