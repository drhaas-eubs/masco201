F41 · Slibrary 11 · Sheet 1/4

Stakeholder Theory

Value is created with, and for, every stakeholder

Source (Harvard): Freeman, R.E. (1984) Strategic Management: A Stakeholder Approach. Boston, MA: Pitman.

In one sentenceOrganisations succeed by creating value for everyone who affects or is affected by them — employees, customers, suppliers, financiers, communities and regulators — not by serving shareholders alone.
About the author(s)R. Edward Freeman (b. 1951) is a professor at the University of Virginia's Darden School of Business. A philosopher by training, he is widely regarded as the founder of stakeholder theory and has written extensively on business ethics.
Guiding question (Hafenwerk Logistics case)FlowPlan affects shift workers in Gdańsk, the Rotterdam works council, the furniture retailer worth 31% of revenue, the vendor and residents near the port: what does each give Hafenwerk, and what value must the rollout create for them?
F42 · Slibrary 11 · Sheet 2/4

Stakeholder Salience Model

Power, legitimacy and urgency decide who gets attention

Source (Harvard): Mitchell, R.K., Agle, B.R. and Wood, D.J. (1997) 'Toward a theory of stakeholder identification and salience: defining the principle of who and what really counts', Academy of Management Review, 22(4), pp. 853-886.

In one sentenceLeaders pay most attention to stakeholders who have power, a legitimate claim and an urgent demand; the more of these three a group holds, the more it must be prioritised.
About the author(s)Ronald K. Mitchell, Bradley R. Agle and Donna J. Wood are management scholars who worked on stakeholder theory, business ethics and corporate social performance. Their 1997 article is among the most cited works in stakeholder research.
Guiding question (Hafenwerk Logistics case)The Rotterdam works council demands consultation before further rollout and the retailer's renewal is six months away: which Hafenwerk stakeholders are definitive, dangerous or dependent today, and whose salience is about to change?
F43 · Slibrary 11 · Sheet 3/4

Media Richness Theory

Match the richness of the channel to the message

Source (Harvard): Daft, R.L. and Lengel, R.H. (1986) 'Organizational information requirements, media richness and structural design', Management Science, 32(5), pp. 554-571.

In one sentenceAmbiguous, emotional or high-stakes messages need rich channels such as face-to-face or video conversations, while routine information can travel through lean channels such as email or written reports.
About the author(s)Richard L. Daft is an organisation theorist and long-standing professor at Vanderbilt University's Owen Graduate School of Management, known for widely used textbooks on organisation theory and leadership. Robert H. Lengel co-developed media richness theory with him in the 1980s.
Guiding question (Hafenwerk Logistics case)Lena de Vries informs the works council and three sites mainly through a monthly video update and written FAQs: which of her FlowPlan messages are too ambiguous for these channels, and where would oversimplification cost her trust?
F44 · Slibrary 11 · Sheet 4/4

Virtual Distance

Physical, operational and affinity distance in hybrid work

Source (Harvard): Sobel Lojeski, K. and Reilly, R.R. (2008) Uniting the Virtual Workforce: Transforming Leadership and Innovation in the Globally Integrated Enterprise. Hoboken, NJ: John Wiley & Sons.

In one sentenceRemote work creates three kinds of distance — physical, operational and emotional affinity — and the affinity gap in shared values, relationships and trust does the most damage to collaboration.
About the author(s)Karen Sobel Lojeski, who earned her PhD at Stevens Institute of Technology, teaches in the Department of Technology and Society at Stony Brook University and founded a firm specialising in virtual distance. Richard R. Reilly was a professor at Stevens Institute of Technology working on organisational behaviour and technology management.
Guiding question (Hafenwerk Logistics case)De Vries visits Gdańsk and Porto only once a quarter, and Porto's team keeps parallel spreadsheets: which layer of virtual distance explains each site's reaction to FlowPlan, and which layer should she reduce first?

Slibrary 12 — Technology for Stakeholder Engagement4 frameworks

F45 · Slibrary 12 · Sheet 1/4

Media Synchronicity Theory

Convey asynchronously, converge synchronously

Source (Harvard): Dennis, A.R., Fuller, R.M. and Valacich, J.S. (2008) 'Media, tasks, and communication processes: a theory of media synchronicity', MIS Quarterly, 32(3), pp. 575-600.

In one sentenceSharing new information works best through channels people can read in their own time, while agreeing on meaning works best in live conversation — so leaders should sequence both deliberately.
About the author(s)Alan R. Dennis is a professor of information systems at Indiana University's Kelley School of Business. Joseph S. Valacich and Robert M. Fuller are information-systems scholars whose work focuses on collaboration technology and human–computer interaction.
Guiding question (Hafenwerk Logistics case)De Vries must agree a revised FlowPlan rollout with three sites and the works council within three weeks: which steps should be conveyed asynchronously, and which conversations must be live to converge on a decision?
F46 · Slibrary 12 · Sheet 2/4

Technology Acceptance Model

People adopt technology they find useful and easy

Source (Harvard): Davis, F.D. (1989) 'Perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use, and user acceptance of information technology', MIS Quarterly, 13(3), pp. 319-340.

In one sentencePeople start using a new technology when they believe it will help them do their job and that it will be easy to use; leaders can strengthen both beliefs.
About the author(s)Fred D. Davis is an information-systems scholar who developed the Technology Acceptance Model during his doctorate at MIT. He has held professorships at several US business schools and later extended the model with Viswanath Venkatesh.
Guiding question (Hafenwerk Logistics case)Porto's team quietly keeps parallel spreadsheets because they do not understand FlowPlan's assignments: is the problem perceived usefulness or perceived ease of use, and what should De Vries change first?
F47 · Slibrary 12 · Sheet 3/4

Explainable AI for Stakeholders

People affected by AI must understand its outputs

Source (Harvard): Gunning, D., Stefik, M., Choi, J., Miller, T., Stumpf, S. and Yang, G.-Z. (2019) 'XAI—Explainable artificial intelligence', Science Robotics, 4(37), eaay7120.

In one sentenceAn AI result that the people affected cannot understand will not earn the right level of trust, so systems should explain why they decided and when they are likely to be wrong.
About the author(s)David Gunning is a computer scientist who managed DARPA's Explainable AI programme. His co-authors include researchers in human–computer interaction, cognitive science and robotics, among them Tim Miller, known for work linking AI explanation to the social sciences.
Guiding question (Hafenwerk Logistics case)Porto's planners cannot see why FlowPlan assigns some shifts and docks as it does: what would an explanation interface need to show them so that Hafenwerk earns appropriate trust rather than parallel spreadsheets?
F48 · Slibrary 12 · Sheet 4/4

AI Stakeholder Listening

AI clusters the signals; the leader judges and responds

Source (Harvard): Mitchell, R.K., Agle, B.R. and Wood, D.J. (1997) 'Toward a theory of stakeholder identification and salience: defining the principle of who and what really counts', Academy of Management Review, 22(4), pp. 853-886.

In one sentenceAI can quickly group what many stakeholders are saying into themes, but leaders must check those themes, decide whose concerns matter most and answer visibly — otherwise listening feels like monitoring.
About the author(s)This is a MASCO201 course adaptation by Dr. Hildegard Haas. It builds on the salience model of Ronald K. Mitchell, Bradley R. Agle and Donna J. Wood (1997) and on research into automated text analysis, which stresses validating machine-generated themes against human reading.
Guiding question (Hafenwerk Logistics case)Two Hafenwerk site managers fear staff will see AI analysis of the project channel and pulse survey as more monitoring: under what conditions could De Vries use it so that it builds trust instead?
