Nordlicht Logistics: Leading the Room Without the Title

A newly hired analyst in Hamburg must win over three senior managers who owe her nothing

Teaching case
Authority is lent, not owned: people follow you only for as long as your character, competence and consistency keep earning it.Core message · Unit 7 · ILO 2, 3

Lena Hartmann, 27, joined Nordlicht Logistics in Hamburg four months ago as a business analyst in the transformation office. Nordlicht, a mid-sized freight forwarder with around 1,400 employees, has lost two large retail customers in a year because delivery-time estimates were wrong almost one time in five. Lena’s analysis shows why: the three regional depots each plan routes with their own spreadsheets, and none of them shares data with the others.

The managing director has asked Lena to present a proposal for a shared planning tool to the monthly operations meeting. She has ten minutes. In the room will be the three depot managers — Jens Albrecht (Hamburg, 22 years with the company), Marta Kowalska (Poznań, the best performer) and Pieter de Vries (Rotterdam, openly sceptical of ‘head-office projects’). None of them reports to Lena, and the managing director has made clear that he will not impose the tool: ‘If the depot managers do not want it, it will fail anyway.’

Lena’s numbers are strong. She has modelled that a shared tool would cut late deliveries from 18% to under 8% and save roughly €640,000 a year in penalties and empty kilometres. But she has also heard the corridor talk. Jens thinks she is ‘a consultant who has never loaded a lorry’. Pieter has already sent an email to colleagues asking why Rotterdam should give up a system that works for Rotterdam. Marta has said nothing, but her depot would lose the most autonomy.

A colleague suggests that Lena should let an AI tool write the presentation and produce a polished video summary to send in advance, ‘so they see how professional it is’. Another tells her to ask the managing director to open the meeting by endorsing her, ‘so they know who is behind you’. Lena is unsure. She knows that she has expert and informational power, but almost no referent power yet, and she suspects that borrowing the director’s legitimate power might win the meeting and lose the depots.

Two weeks before the meeting, Lena still has time to spend a day in each depot. She also knows that the first ten minutes will set how she is seen for the rest of her time at Nordlicht.

Your tasks

  1. Map Lena’s current power bases with French & Raven. Which are positional, which personal, and which could she build in two weeks?
  2. Diagnose her credibility with Covey’s four cores. Where is each depot manager likely to doubt her?
  3. Should she accept the director’s opening endorsement or the AI-produced video? Argue with the rhetorical triangle and the Liar’s Dividend.
  4. Draft her one-sentence core message and the first 60 seconds of her pitch, naming the ethos, pathos and logos in each part.
  5. Decide: what exactly should Lena do in the two weeks before the meeting, and in which order? Justify in 150 words.

Composite teaching case written for MASCO201. Nordlicht Logistics, its people and all figures are illustrative and do not describe a real company.
