Nordhafen Logistics: The Manager Nobody Asked For

A Hamburg logistics firm must decide what its new regional leader is actually for

Teaching case
Classical leadership theories are hypotheses about people, not answers; effective leaders test them against the situation in front of them.Core message · Unit 1 · ILO 1, 4

Nordhafen Logistics is a family-owned contract-logistics company based in Hamburg, running six warehouses across northern Germany and the Netherlands. In the last eighteen months the firm has introduced an AI-based planning system that schedules shifts, forecasts volumes and produces the weekly performance reports that site managers used to compile by hand.

The system works. Forecast accuracy has improved, overtime has fallen and the reports arrive every Monday at 06:00 without anyone touching them. The executive board now faces a question it did not expect. The regional operations manager for the Netherlands, Pieter, is retiring, and the role description written in 2019 is mostly a list of tasks the system now performs: planning rosters, monitoring productivity, compiling reports and escalating deviations.

The chief financial officer, Katrin, proposes not to replace Pieter at all. Site managers would report directly to Hamburg, and the AI dashboard would provide the oversight. She calculates a saving of roughly €140,000 a year including on-costs. The chief operating officer, Jens, disagrees. He points to rising staff turnover at two Dutch sites, a works council that has asked twice for consultation about the planning system, and a key retail customer in Rotterdam that renewed its contract only after a personal visit from Pieter.

The managing director, Anna, asks the HR team to produce a new role profile. The first draft, generated with an AI assistant, describes a 'transformational leader with strategic vision, emotional intelligence and digital fluency'. Anna finds it impossible to disagree with and therefore useless. She wants to know which leadership behaviours the company actually needs from a person, which the system already covers, and whether the role is a management job, a leadership job or neither.

Exit interviews from the two sites with the highest turnover mention three themes: shift changes communicated only through the app, no one to raise concerns with, and a feeling that the company 'runs on autopilot'. Productivity at those sites is still within target.

Anna has asked for a recommendation for the board meeting in three weeks. The options on the table are to abolish the role, to replace it like for like, or to redesign it.

Your tasks

  1. Classify the 2019 role description using Yukl's four behaviour families. Which parts does the AI system already perform?
  2. Is the problem Nordhafen faces technical or adaptive in Heifetz's sense? Justify your answer.
  3. Critique the AI-generated role profile: which leadership theory does it implicitly rely on, and what does it miss?
  4. Which behaviours from the transformational and manager-vs-leader frameworks would address the turnover evidence?
  5. Recommend to the board: abolish, replace or redesign. Write the redesigned role in no more than 150 words.
