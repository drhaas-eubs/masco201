Hafenwerk Logistics: Rolling Out Across Three Ports

An operations director in Rotterdam must win stakeholders she rarely meets in person

Teaching case
In hybrid work, stakeholders judge your leadership by the channel you choose before they hear what you say.Core message · Unit 6 · ILO 3

Hafenwerk Logistics is a mid-sized contract-logistics company with 1,400 employees across three port sites: Rotterdam, Gdańsk and Porto. The executive team has approved FlowPlan, an AI-based planning platform that assigns warehouse shifts, dock slots and truck routes. It promises a 12% cut in empty truck kilometres and fewer late deliveries for the company's largest customer, a Scandinavian furniture retailer that accounts for 31% of revenue.

Lena de Vries, Director of Operations, is responsible for the rollout. She works from Rotterdam two days a week and from home the rest; the Gdańsk and Porto site managers report to her but she visits each site only once a quarter. Most of her stakeholder contact happens on video calls, in a shared project channel and by email.

Six weeks into the project, problems are surfacing in places she cannot easily see. In Gdańsk, shift leaders say FlowPlan's schedules ignore informal arrangements that let parents swap shifts; absenteeism has risen by four percentage points in the pilot area. In Rotterdam, the works council has written to the board stating that it was not properly consulted on a system that monitors and assigns staff, and it expects to be involved before any further rollout. In Porto, the site manager is enthusiastic, but his team has quietly kept parallel spreadsheets because they do not understand why the system makes some assignments.

Outside the company, the furniture retailer's logistics director has asked for a weekly performance report and has hinted that the contract renewal in six months depends on reliability. The vendor wants a reference case and is pushing for full rollout by spring. The port authority in Rotterdam has begun a consultation on truck traffic, and local residents have complained about night-time congestion near the site.

De Vries has so far communicated mainly through a monthly all-site video update and written FAQs. Her project team has suggested using an AI tool to analyse comments from the project channel and a staff pulse survey, so that she can see emerging concerns earlier. Two site managers worry that staff will see this as further monitoring.

The CEO has asked De Vries to present a revised rollout plan in three weeks. She needs the support of the works council, the site teams and the retailer, and she needs it without being in the same room as most of them.

Your tasks

  1. Identify all stakeholders and classify them with the Stakeholder Salience Model. Who is definitive, who is dangerous, and whose salience is likely to change?
  2. Diagnose the virtual distance between De Vries and each site. Which type — physical, operational or affinity — matters most, and why?
  3. Using media richness and media synchronicity, redesign her engagement: which conversations must be live, which can be asynchronous?
  4. Should she use the AI listening tool? Under what conditions would it build trust rather than feel like monitoring?
  5. Decide: what are the three first actions in De Vries' revised rollout plan? Justify them in 150 words.
