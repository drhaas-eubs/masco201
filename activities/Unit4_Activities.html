Business Simulation — Crisis Room
Core message · Unit 4
Most leadership failures in a crisis come from solving the wrong kind of problem quickly and confidently.
  • Under pressure, leaders reach for technical fixes because they are fast, visible and within their authority.
  • Yet the hardest problems require people to change values, habits or loyalties, and no expert can do that work for them.
  • This unit trains you to diagnose the problem type, choose a decision mode that fits the context, and keep trust while you adapt.
Intended learning outcomes addressed
1Critically discuss the approaches to contemporary strategic leadership in organizations.
4Demonstrate the significance of leading strategically during periods of change and turbulence.
Core message distilled after Bostelaar (2024), Clarify Your Core Message: decompose, prioritise, three sentences, one sentence.
Session question
When the complication hits, do you reach for a fix — or for the people who must change?
1
Boostcamp wrap-up: Under Pressure
15 min

Plenary: which decision trap from the Boostcamp episode did you recognise in yourself? Contributions are sorted on the board under 'technical reflex' and 'adaptive avoidance'.

Output: Board of personal decision traps

2
Diagnose: technical or adaptive?
20 min

In pairs, students classify six short episodes of real leadership failure as technical, adaptive or mixed, and name who would have had to do the work.

  1. Classify each episode
  2. Name the loss the adaptive part demanded
  3. Identify the technical fix that was chosen instead

Output: Diagnosis sheet

3
Crisis Room — Round 1
35 min

Teams of 3–4, mirroring Assessment 3, receive the Valcortese Dairy case and act as the CEO's crisis cell.

  1. Locate the Cynefin domain for Friday night and for Monday
  2. Separate technical problems from the adaptive challenge
  3. Set a level of automation for each key decision
  4. Draft the first public holding statement using SCCT

Output: First-response plan and holding statement

Break · 15 minutes
4
Crisis Room — Round 2: the unannounced complication
30 min

Without warning, the facilitator injects a complication card, as the panel will in Assessment 3. Teams have ten minutes to adapt, then two minutes each to defend the revised recommendation. The facilitator directs at least one follow-up question to an individual team member.

Output: Revised plan, defended live

5
AI red-team pre-mortem
20 min

Teams run the AI Red-Team Pre-Mortem on their revised plan: own causes first, then the AI as adversary, then filter to the top three risks with owners. The stakeholder statement is re-checked against SCCT.

Output: Top three risks with owners and AI declaration

6
Balcony debrief and crucible link
15 min

Each student steps onto the balcony: what was adaptive in this crisis, and what did they personally avoid? They then choose a real experience of their own as the 'crucible' for the Assessment 2 Heifetz analysis and write its first paragraph.

Output: Draft crucible paragraph (input for the Assessment 2 dossier)

Deliverable: Per team: a defended one-page crisis plan stating the Cynefin domain, the technical and adaptive work, automation levels per decision, the stakeholder statement and the top three risks. Per student: a draft crucible paragraph for the Assessment 2 dossier.

Materials

  • Valcortese Dairy case pack
  • Complication cards (facilitator only)
  • Cynefin and Levels of Automation worksheet (A3)
  • SCCT response grid
  • Laptops with an approved AI tool
  • AI declaration box on every worksheet
  • Timer and bell

On-screen study only — copying, downloading and printing are disabled.
