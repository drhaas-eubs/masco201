Case Study Duel — Spot the Style
Core message · Unit 3
The style you believe you lead with matters less than the style your people experience when you are under pressure.
  • Most leaders rely on one or two default styles and believe they are far more flexible than they are.
  • Under pressure those defaults narrow further, and the people around you quietly stop telling you what you need to hear.
  • This unit trains you to diagnose your own emotional intelligence and style range, and to see the speaking-up climate you personally create.
Intended learning outcomes addressed
1Critically discuss the approaches to contemporary strategic leadership in organizations.
2Develop a comprehensive leadership development plan to enhance their own career prospects.
Core message distilled after Bostelaar (2024), Clarify Your Core Message: decompose, prioritise, three sentences, one sentence.
Session question
Which style do you reach for under pressure — and what does it cost the people around you?
1
Boostcamp wrap-up: Leading with Empathy
15 min

Pairs name one moment from the Boostcamp episode where empathy changed an outcome. The plenary collects three and links each to one of Goleman's five components.

Output: Three empathy moments mapped to EI components

2
EI self-scan
20 min

Individual, silent work on Goleman's five components.

  1. Rate yourself 1–5 on each component
  2. Write one concrete behavioural example per rating
  3. Mark the component you would least like your team to rate

Output: Personal EI profile (input for the Assessment 2 dossier)

3
Case Study Duel: Spot the Style
35 min

Two teams receive the same three anonymised leadership episodes. Team A classifies them by hand; Team B runs the AI Style-Spotting Protocol. Each team defends its classification for three minutes per episode; the other team challenges with evidence from the text.

  1. Classify each episode against the six styles
  2. Quote the lines that prove the style
  3. Duel: defend, challenge, concede or hold
  4. Note where the AI and the humans disagreed

Output: Classification sheet with evidence lines and AI declaration

Break · 15 minutes
4
Six Styles self-assessment
25 min

Students complete the Six Leadership Styles self-assessment that forms part of the Assessment 2 dossier, then compare it with how they acted in the duel.

  1. Identify your dominant, back-up and missing styles
  2. Name the situation in which you overuse your dominant style
  3. Draft three questions for your 360-degree respondents

Output: Six Styles profile and draft 360 questions

5
Speaking-up audit
30 min

Each student recalls a moment when someone stayed silent with them, or when they stayed silent with a boss, and names the implicit voice theory at work. In trios, one student replays the moment as leader using Edmondson's three moves; the others give feedback on the response to bad news.

Output: One leader behaviour to change, written as a commitment

6
Commitment and declaration
10 min

Each student writes the one style they will practise in the coming week and declares how AI was used in the session.

Output: Signed commitment card with AI declaration

Deliverable: A one-page Style & Voice Profile for the Assessment 2 dossier: EI self-scan, Six Styles self-assessment (dominant, back-up and missing styles), three 360-degree questions and one speaking-up commitment.

Materials

  • Six Leadership Styles self-assessment sheet
  • EI self-scan template
  • Three anonymised case episodes (A4)
  • Laptops with an approved AI tool
  • AI declaration box on every worksheet
  • Commitment cards
  • Timer

On-screen study only — copying, downloading and printing are disabled.
