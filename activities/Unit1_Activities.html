Debate — Theory Tribunal
Core message · Unit 1
Classical leadership theories are hypotheses about people, not answers; effective leaders test them against the situation in front of them.
  • Leadership theory has moved from traits to behaviour, contingency, transformation and adaptation, and each school captured something real.
  • Yet no single theory survives contact with a volatile, AI-shaped workplace in which tasks are automated and the problems that remain are human ones.
  • This unit trains students to reduce each theory to its first premises, test it against evidence and choose deliberately what to use.
Intended learning outcomes addressed
1Critically discuss the approaches to contemporary strategic leadership in organizations.
4Demonstrate the significance of leading strategically during periods of change and turbulence.
Core message distilled after Bostelaar (2024), Clarify Your Core Message: decompose, prioritise, three sentences, one sentence.
Session question
Which leadership theory would you trust to guide you through next Monday, and on what evidence?
1
Hook: the manager an AI could replace
15 min

Show a week in the diary of a regional operations manager. Students mark each entry as task, relations, change or external work and estimate which entries an AI assistant could already do.

Output: Annotated diary with an AI-exposure estimate

2
Mini-lecture: five eras of leadership theory
20 min

Walk through the evolution from trait to adaptive leadership using Yukl. Introduce Heifetz's distinction between technical and adaptive problems as the question the course will return to in Unit 4.

3
Tribunal preparation with first principles
30 min

Teams are assigned one theory (trait, behavioural, contingency, transformational, manager-vs-leader). Applying Aristotle's first-principles method from MASCO101 Unit 3, they reduce the theory to its core premises and test each premise against evidence.

  1. List the theory's three foundational premises
  2. Ask an AI assistant for the strongest critique of each premise and log the prompt
  3. Check the critique against Yukl and one academic source
  4. Prepare a 3-minute defence and a 1-minute concession

Output: Premise sheet with evidence and AI log

Break · 15 minutes
4
The Tribunal
40 min

Each team defends its theory before a jury of peers. A cross-examining team attacks one premise. The jury rules: keep, adapt or retire the theory for leading in an AI-shaped workplace.

  1. 3 minutes defence
  2. 2 minutes cross-examination
  3. 1 minute concession
  4. Jury verdict with one sentence of reasoning

Output: Verdict board: keep / adapt / retire

5
Synthesis: the contemporary leader
20 min

Combine the verdicts into a class profile of the effective contemporary leader. Map it onto the Leadership Task × AI Map and identify which behaviours stay distinctly human.

Output: Class profile of the contemporary leader

6
Declaration and Boostcamp briefing
10 min

Each team declares in one line how AI was used and where it was overruled. Brief the Boostcamp episode “From Manager to Leader” to be completed before Unit 2.

Deliverable: One premise sheet per team with the jury verdict, the evidence used and an AI-use declaration.

Materials

  • Diary handout of a regional operations manager
  • Premise sheet template (A3)
  • Yukl (2020), chapters 1–2
  • Laptops with an approved AI tool
  • Timer and verdict board

On-screen study only — copying, downloading and printing are disabled.
